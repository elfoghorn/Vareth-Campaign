OP.02 // HOUSE RULESWhat's Different Here

Bonus License Ranks

Standard Lancer gives you one license rank per License Level. In this campaign, optional side contracts grant additional license ranks tied to specific manufacturers — representing Atlas engineers reverse-engineering whatever you drag back.

This stacks with normal levelling; it doesn't replace it. Core bonuses still work normally (three ranks in a manufacturer, chosen at LL3/6/9/12). It means the side contracts you take genuinely shape what your frame can do.

Dismount Missions

Some objectives are too small, too unstable, or too delicate for a frame. You will spend real session time on foot, without your mech — collapsed structures, tight corridors, infiltration work.

Your pilot's skill triggers, gear, and nerve carry these scenes. Build a pilot who is interesting outside the cockpit.

Alternate Routes

Optional contracts don't just give gear — each one opens a different approach to a later main mission. A minefield you've thermally mapped is a different problem than one you're walking into blind. Skipping side work is always allowed; it just means doing things the hard way.

Reputation

Every organisation on Vareth tracks how it sees you: Unknown → Contact → Asset → Liability → Target. You won't see a number. You'll see it in how people talk to you, what work you're offered, and who stops returning your calls.

OP.03 // DOWNTIMEThe Half of the Game That Isn't Missions

Roughly 40% of this campaign happens between deployments. Downtime isn't a scene transition here — it's where the story gets built. Some sessions have no mission at all.

Each downtime period, your pilot takes two actions. A partial menu:

Personal

  • Visit someone. Ask how they're doing.
  • Rest properly
  • Work on a hobby or craft
  • Explore Site Zero
  • Write home

Social

  • Drink at the Undertow
  • Attend a base event or a funeral
  • Gamble, badly or otherwise

Professional

  • Work on your frame with Halda
  • Requisition run
  • Train with Contract Security
  • Take a side contract
  • Build reputation with a division

Investigation

  • Research Old War records
  • Talk to a veteran contractor
  • Follow a rumour
Worth Knowing NPCs at Site Zero have lives that continue whether or not you engage with them. People get promoted, get hurt, get transferred, and occasionally stop being at their usual post. Checking in on someone is a mechanically meaningful act.

OP.04 // TABLESafety & Expectations

This campaign deals with corporate exploitation, moral compromise, casualty statistics treated as accounting, and the long aftermath of a war. It isn't gratuitous, but it isn't light.

  • We use lines and veils. Tell the GM before session one, privately if you prefer.
  • Anyone can call for a scene to be paused, rewound, or skipped, at any time, without justifying it.
  • Your pilot can be compromised. Your pilot cannot be forced into a choice you're not comfortable playing.
  • Nobody at this table is required to be the good guy. Nobody is required to be a bastard either.
